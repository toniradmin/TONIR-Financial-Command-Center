Auditor's Guide & Manual

TONIR Setup Philosophy & Reference Documentation

For New Auditors: Where to Start

First time setup:

Fill out Income with every paycheck, benefit, or recurring income source. Enter all fixed obligations in Insurance, Loans (include Buy Now Pay Later), Legal, Mortgage_Rent, Utilities, Subscription_Services, and Allotments (including healthcare, voluntary pay deductions, or survivor benefits). Only add Credit Cards if you carry a balance. Finally, populate Assets and Liabilities with your current bank balances, investments, and long-term debt to capture your starting Net Financial Position. This initial setup alone gives you an accurate TONIR score and net position even if you never track individual purchases.

Reality check:

Logging every transaction to the penny is powerful but most people burn out within 60 days. That is normal and expected. The good news is your TONIR score works without transaction level tracking because fixed obligations drive the majority of your ratio. Groceries and fuel are variable but predictable enough that monthly totals keep your score honest.

Sustainable routine:

When you have energy for detailed tracking use it. Log purchases in Food and Shopping and Fuel to see what drives your variable costs. When life gets busy fall back to entering monthly totals only. Your TONIR score will still be accurate. You just lose the ability to trace which specific purchases moved the needle.

Monthly minimum:

Enter actual amounts for fixed bills when they arrive. Check the TONIR Dashboard for your zone status. That takes 15 minutes and keeps your score current.

Quarterly review:

Review your individual category sections to spot trends. If your TONIR is climbing, identify which category shifted. If you stopped tracking transactions during a busy period, restart without guilt. The system waits for you. Use this quarterly checkpoint to update your balances in Assets and Liabilities so your Net Financial Position on the Dashboard stays current.

Why honesty matters:

TONIR runs on the honor system. Nobody verifies your entries. No bank feeds. No automated syncing. No algorithm checks your work. That means you can lie to yourself if you want to. Leave out the Amazon habit. Forget the takeout twice a week. Exclude the BNPL payment you regret. The number will look better and you will feel better and you will be no closer to financial stability than before. The entire value of TONIR is that it tells you the truth about your situation. The moment you start selectively omitting expenses the score becomes fiction and you are lying to the only person who matters. Yourself.

Every dollar you spend belongs somewhere in this system. The detail you track is your choice. The truth of your score is your responsibility.

Section Descriptions

TONIR Dashboard (The Command Center)

Combines all data to calculate your Total Obligations to Net Income Ratio. Enter your income totals and obligation totals from the other sections. The dashboard automatically computes your TONIR score and shows which stability zone you are in based on your percentage.

1. Income (Where Money Comes From)

Log all income sources here with projected versus actual amounts in both net and gross terms. Include every paycheck, pension, benefit, or side income. Mark which income is recurring versus one-time.

2. Assets and Liabilities (Balance Sheet)

Tracks your investable liquid assets and formal debts to establish a baseline picture of your net financial position. Data is manually input and updated periodically to maintain absolute accuracy and intentionality.

3. Credit Cards

For credit cards where you carry a balance month to month. If you pay your card in full each month it does not count toward your TONIR because no ongoing obligation exists. Captures real obligations that lenders expect you to pay.

4. Food and Shopping

Track every purchase for food, household items, and any non-rent spending. Each dollar entered reveals what is actually consuming your income with no blind spots.

5. Fuel

Log fuel purchases with vehicle and cost descriptions. Fuel costs change frequently, so tracking shows which trips or vehicles strain your TONIR.

6. Investments

Record Roth IRA and 401k contributions separately. While these build wealth, they are committed outflows affecting your TONIR. Knowing the exact contribution amount helps you adjust if needed.

7. Insurance

List each policy with its exact monthly premium; including auto, home or renters, life, dental, and vision. Rates sneak up over time.

8. Legal

Child support and childcare costs logged per payment. Legal obligations are non-negotiable, but documenting each transaction ensures nothing slips through.

9. Loans

Track all loan payments and Buy Now Pay Later arrangements with notes on interest rate and payoff timeline. BNPL schemes count as obligations because they create recurring monthly payments until paid off.

10. Mortgage or Rent

List rent, service fees, transaction fees, and any other housing related charges separately. Hidden costs like maintenance fees or pet charges matter.

11. Subscription Services

List all subscriptions with renewal flags. Services like streaming software or memberships drain money slowly if ignored.

12. Utilities

Record electricity, water, sewage, trash, internet, and cell phone bills to the cent. Itemized tracking tells you which utility is raising your obligations ratio.
